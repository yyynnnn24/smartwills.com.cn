What is a Will?

A Will is a legally binding document that clearly records how you want your assets to be distributed after your passing.

It also allows you to appoint an executor, name guardians for minor children, and leave gifts to selected beneficiaries or charities.
Preparing a Will gives your loved ones clearer instructions and greater peace of mind.

A Will is more than a legal document—it is a clear plan for your assets, responsibilities and loved ones. With SmartWills China, you can record your wishes, reduce uncertainty and give your family greater peace of mind.

FAQs

Everything you're wondering

Why do I need to make a Will?

A Will allows you to decide how your assets should be distributed, appoint an executor, and name a guardian for your minor children.

Without a valid Will, your estate will be distributed according to the applicable law, which may result in a longer and more complicated administration process for your loved ones.

Can I appoint a SmartWriter as my witness?

Yes, you may request a SmartWriter to act as a witness, subject to availability.

A witness service fee of up to RM100 per witness may apply for each Will processed.

Do I need a lawyer to prepare my Will?

No. Malaysian law does not require a Will to be prepared by a lawyer.

However, the Will must be in writing, properly signed and witnessed, and comply with the applicable legal requirements to be valid.

Is a SmartWills online Will legally valid?

Yes. A SmartWills online Will can become legally valid once it is correctly completed, printed, signed, and witnessed in accordance with Malaysian law.

The SmartWills Will template was prepared by panel lawyers and reviewed by its in-house legal adviser.

What assets can I include in my Will?

You may include assets such as property, bank savings, investments, vehicles, shares, jewellery and digital assets.

Your Will may also cover eligible future assets acquired after it is prepared.

What happens if I pass away without a Will?

Your estate will be distributed according to the applicable law instead of your personal wishes.

Your family may also face a longer and more complicated process when appointing an administrator and managing the distribution of your estate.
